# Morning Wave — Emotional Design Language & Home Experience

Design the Morning Wave app around one core emotional idea:

> **“A good morning from Mom, every day.”**

This is **not a medical app, monitoring dashboard, productivity app, or surveillance tool**. It should feel like a warm family ritual translated into a digital experience.

The interface should make the user feel **safe, loved, connected, reassured, and gently cared for**.

## 1. Core Design Philosophy

The design language must prioritize **emotional design over information density**.

Every screen should answer:

> “Does this feel like something made by a family member who cares?”

Avoid the visual language of:
- Medical dashboards
- Elder-care monitoring systems
- Productivity apps
- Corporate SaaS
- Security/alarm systems
- Data-heavy health apps
- Cold notification interfaces

Instead, use the visual language of:
- Morning sunlight
- Handwritten family notes
- Warm photographs
- Gentle celebrations
- Personal messages
- Familiar rituals
- Calm reassurance
- Human connection

The app should feel **alive and emotionally responsive**, not like a database.

---

# 2. Emotional Design Principles

### Warmth

Use a soft sunrise-inspired visual system.

The palette should feel like:
- Early morning sunlight
- Warm cream paper
- Soft peach
- Muted yellow
- Gentle sky blue
- Warm green accents

Avoid extremely saturated colors.

The interface should have a soft, almost tactile quality.

### Human Presence

Whenever possible, represent information through **people and relationships rather than statistics**.

Instead of:

> Check-in status: Successful

Prefer:

> ☀️ Mom said good morning

Instead of:

> Last activity: 8:14 AM

Prefer:

> Mom's morning started at 8:14 AM ☀️

Instead of:

> No check-in detected

Never expose this language to the parent.

The parent's experience should never make them feel like they are being evaluated or monitored.

---

# 3. Home Screen Is the Emotional Center

The home screen should be the strongest and most memorable part of the entire application.

It should **not look like a conventional dashboard**.

The home screen should be built around a collection of large, emotionally meaningful widgets/cards rather than rows of data.

Prioritize **large visual widgets with personality**.

## Parent Home Screen

The parent home screen should be extremely simple.

The primary interaction is:

### ☀️ The Morning Sun

Place one large, beautiful sun/check-in interaction near the center of the screen.

The sun should feel almost physical.

It can have:
- Soft glow
- Subtle breathing animation
- Gentle rays
- Slight movement
- Warm gradient
- Soft shadow
- Tactile press feedback

The user should instinctively understand:

> “I tap the sun to say good morning.”

The interaction should require essentially **one action**.

Do not surround the primary action with unnecessary controls.

The parent should not feel like they are operating software.

They should feel like they are **sending a little “I'm okay” to their family**.

---

# 4. Emotional Home Widgets

The home screen should use a widget/card system.

Widgets should feel like small pieces of a family relationship rather than generic UI components.

### Widget 1 — Morning Greeting

The hero widget.

Example:

> Good morning, Mom ☀️  
> Ready to say hello?

After check-in:

> Good morning, Mom ☀️  
> Your family knows you're up.

Use warm animation and subtle celebration.

---

### Widget 2 — Family Message

A small card showing a recent reaction from the child.

Examples:

> ❤️ Love you, Mom.

> “Have a beautiful day ❤️”

> “Morning Mom! Don't forget your coffee ☕”

The purpose is to reinforce the emotional reward of checking in.

---

### Widget 3 — Family Photo

Display a recent family photo.

Example:

> A little memory from your family ❤️

This should feel more like a photo sitting on a refrigerator than a social-media post.

Use:
- Rounded but organic corners
- Soft shadow
- Large image
- Minimal metadata

---

### Widget 4 — Mood

If enabled, allow the parent to optionally communicate how they feel.

Use expressive visual choices instead of clinical terminology.

For example:

☀️ Great  
🙂 Good  
😐 Okay  
🌧️ Not great

Do not use medical-looking scales.

The interaction should feel optional and lightweight.

---

### Widget 5 — Voice Note

Allow the parent to send a short voice message.

Present it emotionally rather than technically.

Instead of:

> Record audio

Use:

> 🎙️ Send your voice

After recording:

> “A little hello for the family ❤️”

The UI should emphasize the human connection.

---

### Widget 6 — “I'm Away”

A calm, friendly control for temporarily pausing check-ins.

Example:

> Going somewhere?

> Let your family know you're away.

Avoid language such as:

> Disable monitoring  
> Pause alerts  
> Suspend tracking

The feature should feel like communicating with family, not manipulating a monitoring system.

---

# 5. Child Home Screen

The child-facing home should communicate **reassurance**.

The primary emotional question is:

> “How is Mom?”

The answer should be immediately obvious.

Hero card:

> ☀️ Mom said good morning  
> Today · 8:14 AM

This should be visually dominant.

Do not immediately show graphs, percentages, logs, or technical information.

The first thing the child should feel is:

> “Mom is okay.”

---

# 6. Emotional Status Visualization

Use **human language and visual warmth** rather than status labels.

Avoid:

❌ ACTIVE  
❌ INACTIVE  
❌ MONITORED  
❌ ALERT STATUS  
❌ COMPLIANCE  
❌ MISSED CHECK-IN

Prefer:

❤️ Mom checked in this morning

☀️ Morning hello received

💛 Haven't heard from Mom yet

The system can still maintain technically precise states internally, but the UI should translate those states into human language.

---

# 7. Celebration After Check-In

The check-in interaction should have a tiny emotional reward.

When the parent taps the sun:

1. Sun gently expands.
2. Warm rays appear.
3. Small particles/hearts/sunlight emerge.
4. A subtle haptic feedback occurs.
5. The interface transitions into the completed state.
6. Show:

> **Good morning, Mom ☀️**

Then:

> Your family knows you're okay.

Keep the animation short and elegant.

It should feel like a **small moment of joy**, not a gamification mechanic.

---

# 8. Widget Layout

Use an adaptive vertical card layout.

The hierarchy should be:

1. **Hero emotional interaction**
2. **Family reassurance**
3. **Personal connection**
4. **Optional utilities**

The screen should breathe.

Use generous spacing and avoid packing every available feature onto the screen.

Cards should feel like **objects floating on a warm surface**, not rectangles inside a dashboard.

Use subtle:
- Shadows
- Gradients
- Texture
- Rounded surfaces
- Layering
- Depth
- Micro-interactions

But keep the overall interface calm and uncluttered.

---

# 9. Typography

Typography should feel:
- Friendly
- Human
- Extremely readable
- Soft
- Confident without being corporate

The parent experience must use **large typography**.

Minimum parent body text:

**20sp**

Primary action text:

**28sp+**

Maintain strong contrast and accessibility.

Use large headings with short sentences.

Avoid dense paragraphs.

---

# 10. Motion Design

Motion should communicate emotion, not decoration.

Use:
- Slow breathing animations
- Gentle sunlight movement
- Soft card transitions
- Small celebratory particles
- Tactile button feedback
- Crossfades
- Subtle spring animations

Avoid:
- Fast transitions
- Excessive bouncing
- Aggressive notification animations
- Gamified streaks
- Confetti explosions
- Excessive motion

The overall motion language should feel like:

> **Sunlight moving through a room in the morning.**

---

# 11. Design the Emotional States

Do not only design the default state.

Create distinct visual states for:

### Before Check-In

> Good morning, Mom ☀️  
> Ready to say hello?

### After Check-In

> Good morning, Mom ☀️  
> Your family knows you're okay.

### Family Reaction

> ❤️ Your daughter sent you some love.

### Voice Message

> 🎙️ A little hello from your family.

### Photo

> ❤️ A memory from your family.

### Away Mode

> You're away  
> Your family knows.

### No Response / Escalation

The parent should **never feel blamed or frightened**.

Never display:

> You missed your check-in.

Instead use neutral, supportive language appropriate to the situation.

---

# 12. Visual Personality

The app should have a recognizable visual signature.

A person should be able to see a Morning Wave screenshot without seeing the logo and still recognize:

> “This is Morning Wave.”

Build that identity around:

**Sunlight + warmth + family photos + soft cards + emotional microcopy + generous whitespace.**

Avoid generic Flutter/Material-looking interfaces.

Do not make the app look like a standard CRUD application.

Do not use excessive Material cards, dense AppBars, tables, dashboards, or generic settings screens.

---

# 13. Important Constraint

The emotional design must never compromise clarity.

This is an app used by older adults.

Therefore:

**Emotional ≠ complicated.**

Every emotional interaction must remain:
- Obvious
- Large
- Accessible
- Forgiving
- Easy to understand
- Easy to undo
- Low cognitive load

The parent should be able to open the app and understand what to do **without instructions**.

---

# 14. Overall Design Goal

Imagine the product as a combination of:

**A family photo frame  
+ a morning ritual  
+ a tiny digital hug  
+ a reassuring family connection**

rather than:

**A senior monitoring application.**

The final interface should make the parent feel:

> “My family is thinking about me.”

And make the adult child feel:

> “Mom is okay.”

Every design decision should reinforce those two emotions.
